Why bicycle

As I have observed during my previous "expeditions" traveling by bike is much more than just an adventure; it is a total immersion in nature, culture, and oneself. From rugged mountains to lush valleys, from bike paths to sandy trails, pedaling through diverse landscapes offers a unique and intimate perspective on the world around us. But cycling allows access to places often inaccessible by car, revealing hidden treasures and breathtaking panoramas.


Not to mention that traveling by bike is a beneficial physical activity for health and contributes to reducing our carbon footprint on our fragile ecosystem. Human encounters are another rich aspect of cycling trips. Stopping in villages, chatting with curious locals, sharing impromptu meals and unexpected moments of conviviality, and sleeping in people's homes create authentic bonds and unforgettable memories.


Traveling by bike is also about breaking routines, letting go of attachments, opening the door of our golden prison, and reconnecting with the essential. We take the time to observe wildlife and flora, to capture the scent of the world, from a spicy market to a harvested field, to feel the elements of Mother Nature within and around us, in short, to savor every moment. This disconnection allows us to recharge, to put the small daily hiccups of our well-regulated life into perspective, and to return with a much clearer vision of the world we live in.


Finally, cycling travel is a personal adventure that teaches us autonomy, consent, and patience, and forces us to trust ourselves to face the challenges of the journey, to manage the unexpected, or to overcome our own physical or mental difficulties.


In short, going on a bike trip is a complete experience that nourishes the body, mind, and soul. It is an invitation to discover the world differently, to get closer to nature and others while (re)discovering oneself, and for once, to let the story write and invent itself.
